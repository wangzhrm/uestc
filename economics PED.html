Answer Key

1. c) Demand is inelastic
Explanation: A PED value of 0.2 is less than 1, indicating inelastic demand, where a price change leads to a proportionally smaller change in quantity demanded, as per the PowerPoint's definition of inelastic demand (PED < 1).

2. c) To focus on the magnitude of responsiveness
Explanation: The negative sign is omitted in PED to emphasize the magnitude of the responsiveness rather than the direction, as demand curves typically slope downward, making the negative sign redundant (implied in the PowerPoint's repeated question about the negative sign).

3. b) Scotch Whisky
Explanation: Scotch Whisky has a PED of 4.4 (per the PowerPoint table), the highest among the options, indicating elastic demand due to its luxury nature and availability of substitutes, unlike necessities like insulin, eggs, or medical care.

4. a) 0.5
Explanation: PED = (% change in quantity demanded) / (% change in price) = 5% / 10% = 0.5. This indicates inelastic demand, as the quantity demanded changes by a smaller percentage than the price.

5. c) The good accounts for a large proportion of income
Explanation: Goods that account for a large proportion of income tend to have more elastic demand, as consumers are more sensitive to price changes, as stated in the PowerPoint under determinants of PED.

6. PED = 0.38 (inelastic). Working: % change in quantity = [(7200 - 7000) / 7000] × 100 = 2.86%; % change in price = [(37000 - 40000) / 40000] × 100 = -7.5%; PED = |2.86 / -7.5| = 0.38. Interpretation: Demand is inelastic, so a price decrease leads to a smaller proportional increase in quantity demanded.

7. New Qd = 125. Working: % change in price = [(10 - 8) / 8] × 100 = 25%; % change in Qd = PED × % change in price = 1.5 × 25% = 37.5%; New Qd = 200 × (1 - 0.375) = 125.

8. Demand for addictive goods like cigarettes is price inelastic because consumers with physical dependence are less responsive to price increases, as they prioritize consumption despite higher costs, as explained in the PowerPoint under determinants (addictive or not).

9. Goods that account for a large proportion of income have more elastic demand because consumers are more sensitive to price changes, as these significantly impact their budget. Conversely, goods with a small income proportion have inelastic demand, as price changes have less impact. For example, a price increase for an expensive car is likely to reduce demand more than a price increase for bottled water, as the former takes a larger income share, per the PowerPoint.

10. a) Scotch Whisky (PED = 4.4) has the most elastic demand, likely due to its luxury status and many substitutes, making consumers highly responsive to price changes.
b) % change in Qd = PED × % change in price = 0.5 × 10% = 5% decrease.
c) Eggs (PED = 0.1) are a necessity with few substitutes, making demand inelastic, while dining in a restaurant (PED = 2.3) is a luxury with many alternatives, leading to more elastic demand, per the PowerPoint's determinants and table.

11. A 10% increase in the price of an economy-sized car ($10,000 to $11,000) has a bigger impact than a 10% increase in bottled water ($2.00 to $2.20). Cars account for a larger proportion of income, making demand more elastic, as consumers are more sensitive to the $1,000 increase. Bottled water, a necessity with a small income proportion and many substitutes (e.g., tap water), likely has inelastic demand, so the $0.20 increase has less impact, per the PowerPoint's determinants (proportion of income, necessity, substitutes).
